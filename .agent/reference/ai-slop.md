# **Avoiding AI Slop**

**AI slop** refers to AI-generated content perceived as shallow, low-quality, and mass-produced: a kind of spam for the AI era. Both Merriam-Webster and the American Dialect Society chose “slop” as their 2025 Word of the Year. This guide has a simple goal: to help you write text that sounds like it was written by a person, not a model.

## **Common Signs of AI-Generated Text**

### **Punctuation and Word Choice**

- **Em dashes everywhere**, sometimes called the “ChatGPT dash.” Use them sparingly; prefer commas, periods, or parentheses.
- **Inflated language when simple words would do**: _delve, underscore, highlight, tapestry, boast, leverage, robust, seamless, realm_, and _navigate_ in a figurative sense.
- **“From X to Y”** constructions used as a rhetorical formula.
- Structures such as **“it’s not just X, it’s Y”** or **“not only… but also”** used mechanically.

### **Formulaic Language**

- **Hedging or transition phrases**: “it’s important to note that,” “in general,” “to some extent,” “from a broader perspective,” “in today’s fast-paced world.”
- **Empty openings and closings**: generic, wordy sentences that circle around the subject instead of getting to the point.

### **Structure**

- **The same paragraph pattern repeated mechanically**: topic sentence → supporting evidence → summary sentence.
- **Trailing participial phrases**: main clause + comma + an “-ing” phrase, as in “The system analyzes the data, highlighting the key elements.”
- **Too many bullet points** when straightforward prose would be clearer. And, above all, bold text everywhere.
- A **“Conclusion”** or **“In summary”** section that repeats what has just been said.

### **Voice: The Clearest Signal**

- **No personal touch**: concrete details, anecdotes, and specific references are missing.
- **A neutral, inoffensive tone**, always taking the most cautious position possible.
- **An inconsistent voice across texts**: there is no recognizable author behind the writing.

## **Why It Matters**

- **Slop crowds out higher-quality content** and erodes trust. It creates a “market for lemons”: when readers expect AI-generated content, human contributors may stop participating.
- It is often unpleasant to read because of a **mismatch in expectations**: the reader expects a person but perceives a machine instead, creating noticeable friction.
- Online communities such as Hacker News increasingly call out what they consider “AI slop.” Even when the accusation is wrong, it can damage a writer’s credibility.

## **How to Write Without Sounding Like AI Slop**

1. **Be specific.** Use concrete details, real examples, and precise numbers instead of generalizations.
2. **Get to the substance.** Cut unnecessary preambles and conclusions that summarize everything all over again.
3. **Take a position.** Make a clear recommendation without hedging every sentence.
4. **Use simple words.** Replace inflated language with the simplest word that accurately conveys your meaning.
5. **Vary the rhythm.** Mix short and long sentences, and avoid building every paragraph around the same pattern.
6. **Prefer prose to lists**, unless a list really is the clearest way to present the information.
7. **Use em dashes and formulas like “it’s not just X, it’s Y” sparingly.**
8. **Keep a consistent voice.** Be direct and concrete, and cut the filler.
