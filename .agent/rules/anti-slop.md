# **Anti-Slop Rules**

Apply these rules to all prose: documentation, comments, commit messages, PR descriptions, and READMEs. For context and sources, see [reference/ai-slop.md](../reference/ai-slop.md).

1. **No unnecessary preambles.** Cut openings like “In today’s fast-paced world” and closings like “In conclusion.” Get straight to the point.
2. **No inflated language.** Avoid words like _delve, leverage, robust, seamless, tapestry, realm, underscore, highlight, boast_, and _navigate_ in a figurative sense. Use the simplest word that accurately conveys your meaning.
3. **Use em dashes sparingly.** At most one per paragraph. Prefer commas, periods, or parentheses where possible.
4. **Cut filler phrases.** Avoid “it’s important to note that,” “in general,” “to some extent,” “from a broader perspective,” and similar formulas.
5. **Avoid formulaic patterns.** Skip constructions like “it’s not just X, it’s Y” or “from X to Y” when used as generic rhetorical devices.
6. **Be concrete.** Use real examples, precise numbers, and explicit references instead of generalizations.
7. **Take a position.** State your recommendation clearly. Don’t fill every sentence with caveats and hedges.
8. **Prefer prose to lists.** Use bullet points only when they genuinely make a list clearer. Don’t put everything in bold.
9. **No redundant summaries.** Don’t repeat at the end what you just said.
10. **Vary sentence rhythm.** Avoid building every paragraph around the same pattern: topic sentence → evidence → summary.
