# Anti-Slop Review Checklist

Use this checklist after rewriting. Mark each check **pass** or **fail**. If even one check fails, revise the draft before returning it.

For detection requests, identify each pattern found, quote the relevant sentence verbatim, and suggest a brief correction without rewriting the draft.

## Review Principles

1. Does the revision preserve the author’s point of view without adding claims, examples, statistics, quotations, or opinions?
2. Does it preserve the author’s distinctive vocabulary, rhythm, candor, humor, hesitations, digressions, and degree of polish?
3. Does it leave effective, human-sounding sentences intact rather than rewriting them for consistency or polishing every paragraph to the same degree?
4. Are cuts proportionate to the actual problems, without compressing the text so much that it loses its personality?
5. Does the text put what the reader needs first while retaining personal opening details that add context, tension, or character?
6. Are main points brought forward where doing so improves clarity, without forcing every section into the same structure?
7. Does every sentence serve a purpose? Are concrete facts and important details preserved, and are direct verbs used where the text allows?
8. Does every generic sentence pass the transferability test? In other words, could it fit another topic unchanged? If so, has it been removed or made specific to the context?
9. Is the active voice preferred, with explicit human subjects where possible?
10. Does the revision preserve the text’s expressive force and respect its structure, except where that structure undermines its effectiveness?
11. Are genuinely convoluted sentences corrected while preserving natural speech rhythms, sentence fragments, and variations in pace?

## Words and Expressions to Remove

1. Have banned words, filler expressions, often unnecessary adverbs, and emphatic or exaggerated claims been removed, except when they appear in quoted examples?

## Patterns to Remove

1. Have artificial binary contrasts, negative enumerations, introductory rhetorical devices, and openings that stall without adding anything been removed?
2. Have openings that pretend to reveal profound insights, colon-led revelations, superficial analysis, verbs that mimic forcefulness, contrived strings of synonyms, dramatic fragments, and robotic rhythms been corrected?
3. Have exaggerated claims about importance and vague attributions been replaced with clear facts and identified sources, or, where no source is available, has the issue been flagged to the user?
4. Has unnecessary interpretive commentary been removed, including the author’s comments on their own text, instructions to the reader, devices used for emphasis, and redundant explanations?
5. Have closing sentences that pretend to be profound been removed rather than rewritten with more elaborate metaphors?
6. Have conclusions that merely recap what has already been said been removed, so the text ends with a concrete fact, a useful conclusion, or the next action to take?
7. Has unnecessary formatting been removed, including headings with emojis, decorative bolding, lists that would work better as prose, and headings for sections that are too short?
8. Are colons used judiciously, avoiding capitalization after them except where required by grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code?
9. Are em dashes used sparingly? In short texts, normally none; in long texts, at most one or two, and only when they genuinely improve readability.

## Final Check

1. Does the draft avoid artificial symmetry, repeated sentence structures, and strings of short, punchy sentences?
2. Would the author recognize their own voice in the revised version?
3. Would the text sound natural if read aloud to an attentive, perceptive colleague?
4. Does the final response include the complete revised text and a brief section titled **What Changed**?
5. For detection requests, does the response identify each pattern found, quote the relevant passage verbatim, and suggest a brief correction without rewriting the text, assigning scores, or claiming it was written by AI?
